# Hiring Plan — CEO (Trainer)

**Status: Draft v1.** Companion to
[`XOLOKAN_BUSINESS_PLAN.md`](XOLOKAN_BUSINESS_PLAN.md) §6 (Operations &
Scaling), which this plan supersedes for Phase 1 — the original plan
modeled Oscar running Phase 1 solo with AI support and hiring help only at
~50+ subscribers. Oscar has since decided to bring in a CEO from day one
instead: a working trainer who becomes the day-to-day operator and public
face of XOLO FITNESS, while Oscar moves to a backend role (product,
systems, business operations, finance, and strategy). This document is
the concrete hiring plan for that role — who it is, what it owns, how
it's compensated, and how it's screened for.

**One title decision made explicitly, worth restating so it doesn't drift
in casual conversation or later copy: this hire's title is CEO, not
Founder.** Oscar founded XOLO FITNESS and keeps that title and story — the
brand narrative, the storefront's "Method by Oscar Xolo Sifuentes" line,
and XOLOKAN's own persona all stay attributed to him. Calling a hire
"Founder" when they didn't found the company is a claim customers would
reasonably read as false, even informally — keep the language disciplined
everywhere this role gets referenced (job posts, social bios, the
storefront) so that never happens by accident.

---

## 1. Why this role, why now

The business plan's original Phase 1 assumed Oscar personally delivers
the Personalized/Premium tiers' human touch (chat availability, check-ins,
form feedback) while XOLOKAN handles program generation. That's a real
bottleneck the moment volume grows past what one person can serve well —
and Oscar has decided not to wait until that bottleneck actually bites to
solve it. Bringing in a CEO now, pre-revenue, is a bet that having a
dedicated operator and face of the brand from day one is worth the real
cost (§4) versus the opportunity cost of Oscar's own time being split
across product, ops, and client delivery simultaneously.

**A trainer specifically, not a generalist operator** — because the CEO
role here isn't "run the business" in the abstract, it's "be the person
clients actually train with and see on camera," which requires real
coaching competence, not just management skill. The org structure below
reflects that: the CEO owns everything client-facing and brand-facing;
Oscar owns everything the CEO shouldn't have to touch to do that well.

---

## 2. Role split — CEO vs. Founder

Defined explicitly so both people know what they own without asking each
time. When something doesn't clearly fall on one side, default to
whoever's closer to the client, and loop the other person in — that
default favors speed on day-to-day calls and deliberation on structural
ones.

| Area | CEO (Trainer) owns | Founder (Oscar) owns |
|---|---|---|
| Client coaching delivery | Personalized/Premium tier check-ins, form feedback, chat availability, day-to-day client relationships | — |
| Public brand presence | Instagram content, DMs, discovery calls, the face on camera in marketing (§5.3 of the business plan) | Founder story/credibility stays part of the brand narrative, but isn't the day-to-day public presence |
| Training methodology & product | Flags real-world coaching feedback (what's working/not with real clients) back to the Founder | Owns `XOLOKAN_METHODOLOGY.md`, `archetypes.ts`, the program generator, and all methodology edits — the CEO delivers the Method, doesn't unilaterally change it |
| Pricing & business operations | Input on what's working commercially | Final call on pricing, financial tracking, hiring/contracts, vendor and tooling decisions |
| Tech/systems | Uses the tools (XOLOKAN chat, the generator, the PDFs) | Builds and maintains them |
| Strategy | Input from the client-facing vantage point | Sets direction, owns the business plan, has final say on strategic decisions |

**In short: the CEO runs delivery and the brand's public face; the
Founder runs what makes delivery possible and owns where the company is
headed.** Neither role is "senior" to the other in a hierarchy sense —
they're a deliberate division of labor between two different, both
necessary, kinds of work.

## 3. Candidate profile

**Must-haves:**
- Real coaching credibility — a certification and track record that
  would hold up to a client or a camera asking "why should I trust you."
- Comfortable being the on-camera, public-facing presence of a brand —
  this is a real and different skill from being a good 1:1 coach, and
  it's the parts of §5 of the business plan (content, DMs, discovery
  calls) this person owns entirely.
- Genuine alignment with the existing science-based, cited methodology —
  this isn't a role for someone who wants to freelance the programming;
  it's for someone who'll deliver `XOLOKAN_METHODOLOGY.md`'s approach
  faithfully and give good field feedback on it, not override it
  informally with clients.
- Business judgment good enough to run day-to-day operations without
  needing Oscar in every decision — per the role split above, this
  person operates with real autonomy on their side of the line.

**Strong plus, not required:**
- Existing audience or reputation in the dance/gymnastics/performer
  training space — accelerates §5's content-and-trust-building curve
  directly.
- Direct experience with the specific disciplines XOLO FITNESS serves
  (dance, gymnastics/aerial, performance arts) — the methodology is
  discipline-specific, and lived experience in it shows on camera and in
  coaching quality.

**Explicit non-requirement:** business-operator/MBA-style experience.
That's Oscar's side of the split by design (§2) — screening for it here
would optimize for the wrong thing.

## 4. Compensation structure

**This is a bootstrapped, pre-revenue business — the Year-1 revenue
scenarios in `XOLOKAN_BUSINESS_PLAN.md` §4.3 run $3K-$15K MRR by month
12, not venture-funded numbers.** A fixed market-rate CEO salary (which
runs well into six figures at funded startups) isn't a realistic
structure here and would create cash-flow risk neither side wants. The
right shape for this stage is a low-or-no fixed base paired with
revenue-share, so the CEO's income scales with the same growth curve as
the business — genuinely aligned incentives, not a promise the business
can't keep.

**Recommended structure (a starting point for negotiation, not a fixed
offer):**

| Component | Structure | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Base retainer | Modest fixed monthly amount, sized to the Conservative-scenario MRR the business can actually sustain (§4.3) — revisit upward as real revenue clears each scenario tier | Bootstrapped businesses typically pair below-market fixed pay with upside instead of a full market salary; this keeps the business solvent while the CEO is still building the client base they'll earn from |
| Revenue share on tiers they personally deliver | A meaningful percentage of Personalized/Premium-tier revenue for clients they're actively coaching — this is the direct-delivery equivalent of a trainer's session-based pay, restructured for a subscription model | Ties the largest part of comp directly to the work this person actually does day-to-day, not just company-wide performance |
| Company-wide override | A smaller percentage across total company revenue, reflecting the CEO/operator role beyond direct coaching (brand-building, content, ops) | Rewards the parts of the role that grow the whole business, not just their own client roster |
| Equity / long-term ownership stake | A minority equity or profit-share stake, vesting over time (e.g., a multi-year schedule) rather than granted up front | Signals this is a genuine long-term partnership, not just a job — appropriate for someone taking on the CEO title and public identity of the brand. **The exact percentage and vesting terms need real legal/accounting input before anything is offered or signed — this document sets the shape of the offer, not the binding terms.** |

**Do not skip legal/accounting review before finalizing any of this.**
Equity terms, vesting schedules, and whether this role is structured as
an employee or a genuine business partner (with the tax and liability
implications either carries) are exactly the kind of decisions that are
cheap to get right up front and expensive to unwind later. This is true
regardless of how much trust exists between Oscar and the candidate — get
it in writing, reviewed, before day one.

## 5. Screening & interview process

1. **Portfolio/credibility review** — certifications, coaching history,
   any existing content or client results. This is the fastest filter on
   the "real coaching credibility" must-have (§3).
2. **On-camera trial** — a short piece of real content (a form-cue
   breakdown, a program-philosophy explainer) shot and reviewed before an
   offer. Public-facing comfort is a make-or-break requirement for this
   role specifically and it's not reliably assessable from a resume or a
   normal interview conversation.
3. **Methodology alignment conversation** — walk the candidate through
   `XOLOKAN_METHODOLOGY.md`'s actual approach (strength-first injury
   prevention, RIR-based autoregulation, the age/sex programming layer)
   and gauge genuine fit, not just polite agreement. A candidate who
   pushes back thoughtfully on specifics is a better sign than one who
   agrees with everything — it means they'll actually engage with the
   Method rather than nod along and freelance later.
4. **Working session** — have the candidate run a real (or realistic
   mock) client check-in using the actual tools (XOLOKAN chat, a sample
   generated program) to confirm they can operate day-to-day inside the
   system as built, not just in principle.
5. **Reference check specifically on reliability and follow-through** —
   given the CEO owns client relationships and public brand presence
   directly (§2), consistency matters more here than in most hires; a
   brilliant but inconsistent public face is a real brand risk, not just
   a performance issue.

## 6. Onboarding — first 90 days

Mapped directly onto the business plan's existing launch sequence
(`XOLOKAN_BUSINESS_PLAN.md` §5.5), adjusted for the CEO owning it instead
of Oscar:

- **Weeks 1-2**: CEO onboarded to the actual product — runs through the
  program generator, the full PDF catalog, the storefront, and the
  methodology doc end to end. Stripe Payment Links go live
  (`packages/storefront/SETUP.md` — still the first real blocker
  regardless of who's driving). Existing Instagram audience gets
  introduced to the new public face deliberately, not as an afterthought
  — this is a real trust-transfer moment worth planning, not just
  announcing.
- **Weeks 3-6**: CEO running the content cadence and DM funnel from
  business plan §5.4-5.2 under their own name/face. Oscar stays visible
  as Founder in the transition period rather than disappearing abruptly —
  a sudden, unexplained handoff of "who you're actually talking to" reads
  as a red flag to a small, connected audience (business plan §7, risk
  #5).
- **Weeks 7-12**: First real client cohort under the CEO's direct
  delivery. Revisit the comp structure in §4 against real numbers at the
  same 90-day mark the business plan already uses for its own
  assumption-replacement checkpoint (`XOLOKAN_BUSINESS_PLAN.md` §8).

## 7. Risks specific to this hire

- **Trust transfer risk.** The storefront and existing content have
  centered Oscar's personal story and credibility. Moving day-to-day
  delivery to a new person is a real trust-transfer challenge, not just
  an operational handoff — plan the audience-facing transition
  deliberately (§6) rather than treating it as a backend org change
  invisible to clients.
- **Brand-voice consistency.** XOLOKAN's own persona and the
  methodology's cited, careful tone (see `persona.ts` and
  `XOLOKAN_METHODOLOGY.md`) took real iteration to get right — the CEO's
  own on-camera voice needs to feel like the same brand, not a
  different one bolted on. Worth an explicit voice/style conversation
  during onboarding, not an assumption it'll align naturally.
- **Contractor vs. employee classification** — same caution already
  flagged in `XOLOKAN_BUSINESS_PLAN.md` §6 (Phase 2): structure this
  deliberately as a genuine partner/contractor relationship or a real
  employment relationship, not an ambiguous middle. This hire is more
  consequential than the Phase 2 assistant-coach hire that caution was
  originally written for, so the legal review in §4 matters even more
  here.
- **Single point of failure.** Once the CEO is the public face and
  primary coaching-delivery person, the business now has concentration
  risk on two people instead of one. Not a reason not to do this — just
  worth naming as a reason `archetypes.ts`'s documented, systematized
  Method (not tacit knowledge in one person's head) stays valuable
  regardless of who's delivering it, per the Phase 3 multi-coach vision
  already in the business plan.
